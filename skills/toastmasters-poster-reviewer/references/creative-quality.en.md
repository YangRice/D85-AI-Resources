# Creative poster quality

Read for new poster creation, redesign, or a user-supplied aesthetic benchmark. This is a production quality guide, not an expansion of official Toastmasters rules. Brand/content review and aesthetic review are separate judgments.

## Art direction before coordinates

A correct information sheet is not automatically a good event poster. Establish audience, message hierarchy and a visual direction before fixing text boxes. Describe the desired focal point, portrait treatment, atmosphere, reading path and how shapes connect the page. A layout manifest captures the selected design; it must not force the image model into stacked white rectangles or a tiny decorative header.

If the user supplies examples, identify what makes the strongest example work and what makes the weaker one a minimum floor. Borrow principles (scale, integration, rhythm, depth), not its claims, dates, QR patterns or unlicensed imagery. Choose a visual premise from the event theme and build its own composition. Do not prescribe the example’s diagonals, curved masks, portrait placement or footer geometry. Unless the user requests imitation, use finished examples as quality benchmarks only: extract abstract quality criteria and keep them out of the generation inputs. Supply raw authorized materials instead. A loose guide is optional when a complete one-shot proof can establish the design more effectively.

Originality is an acceptance gate: compare the dominant geometry, portrait/headline relationship, reading path, supporting-information structure and footer against the samples. Reusing that spatial skeleton with new colors, copy or slightly different curves is copying, even when the rendering is beautiful. Request a new concept rather than repeatedly editing the borrowed composition.

## Joint quality and production feasibility

Aesthetic quality and the applicable brand/content/protected-material checks must pass on the same final candidate. A beautiful proof does not complete the task. Keep the proof within the brand typography, imagery and gradient limits from the outset; do not build its appeal around dominant decorative digital art or an unapproved Latin font and expect overlays to rescue it later. Check that the composition still works with actual source-photo sharpness, full approved copy, original logos and source QR density. If a compliant reconstruction loses the benchmark quality, redesign within the constraints and re-review both gates rather than lowering the aesthetic target or relaxing brand rules.

## Let the image model design

Use the model's composition and rendering abilities, not merely flat background fill. Supply a concise art-directed brief, authorized references and a loose relational layout. Let it improve spacing, silhouette, depth and shape relationships. Request a complete design proof when useful; model-rendered typography is permitted in a proof so its visual hierarchy can be evaluated. Official logos/QR are exact final overlays, never trusted model approximations.

Choose a production route based on the proof:

- **Strong full composition:** preserve its successful visual language. Produce a clean artwork plate with suitable text/QR/logo areas, then place accurate editable text and protected assets in the same composition. Do not bury the rendering under large opaque panels that recreate a generic grid. If user-approved final bitmap typography is retained, disclose that it is raster text rather than claim full SVG text editability.
- **Good material, weak complete rendering:** generate an attractive composition-aware material/background and integrate the original extracted speaker locally. Authorized matting/cropping may remove the stage rectangle; preserve face/body appearance and original source, record masks/transforms, and inspect hair/hand edges against the actual plate, not only a transparent preview. Stage or lectern remnants and hard alpha corners can ruin an otherwise strong composition; clean the matte rather than regenerate the face. Keep hands outside a broad torso fade where practical; a global fade can erase fingers. Check that bright background shapes do not show through a translucent torso unintentionally—adjust the shape or alpha boundary rather than retouch the person. A visibly pasted rectangular photo or crude torso cutoff does not meet the quality floor. Do not replace a speaker's identity with a generated approximation.

Use image-generation/editing capabilities for visual artwork. Deterministic tools support exact text, original assets, approved extraction/matting, QR safety and necessary palette corrections; they must not substitute a few rectangles for the creative work. Color correction should preserve generated geometry/depth where compliant, and should not consume iteration effort before checking whether the overall design is aesthetically worth keeping.

Private references may be transmitted only within existing user authorization. Sanitized guides remain appropriate when transmission is not authorized. Do not universally exclude an authorized subject/reference image and thereby deprive the model of necessary art-direction context.

## Independent aesthetic review

Use a separate visual-quality reviewer when the user requests one or sets an explicit aesthetic benchmark. Give the actual full/mobile candidate and supplied references; ask for evidence-based comparison on originality first, then:

1. Composition: coherent focal points and reading path, useful negative space, no generic stacked boxes.
2. Portrait integration: meaningful face scale, clean authorized matte/crop, natural edge/shape relationships, no pasted screenshot or accidental truncation.
3. Hierarchy: immediate headline impact, subordinate details, legible essentials.
4. Coherence: type, imagery, shapes and accents feel like one design.
5. Information clarity: comfortable mobile reading; balanced and clearly labeled QR/footer.
6. Finish: deliberate spacing/alignment, no tangencies, poor cutout edges, collisions or muddy rendering.

Fail a copied composition regardless of its finish; explain the shared structural features. Return `MEETS_TARGET`, `MEETS_FLOOR_ONLY`, or `BELOW_FLOOR`, with the reviewed checksum, comparison to each reference, remaining defects and prioritized fixes. These are aesthetic judgments, not claims of official approval or factual/QR validity. Never claim equivalent-to-target quality based only on the designer's own opinion. Continue toward the target when meaningful improvements are available; floor-only is a disclosed intermediate result, not full success when the user requested target quality.

Follow the user's iteration scope. An explicit instruction to refine until a benchmark is met authorizes further creative revisions beyond the ordinary three-attempt default. Keep versioned candidates and review each changed output; do not use a default stop count to leave a user-authorized refinement unfinished. If progress stalls, diagnose the failed route and switch between full-composition rendering and material-plus-matte before seeking missing direction.
